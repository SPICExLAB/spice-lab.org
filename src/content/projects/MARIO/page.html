MARIO: Motion-Augmented Real-Time Multi-Sensor Inertial Odometry

Yiquan Li1,*   Taeyoung Yeon1,*   Chenfeng Gao1   Vasco Xu2   Xuanyou Liu1   Karan Ahuja1
1 Northwestern University    2 University of Chicago    * Equal contribution
Paper PDF Code BibTeX

AbstractInertial odometry that knows how people move

Inertial odometry (IO) using only IMUs is a lightweight way to track a person in AR and wearable devices, but learned IO models still drift because they integrate acceleration without any notion of the body producing it.

MARIO grounds inertial odometry in human kinematics. A learned, IMU-inferred pose prior injects physically consistent motion constraints into existing IO architectures and reduces positional drift by up to 36% on Nymeria, a daily-activity dataset five times larger than those used in prior work. A second stage fuses the magnetometer, barometer and secondary IMU that commercial AR glasses already carry; with it, drift falls by up to 42% and the models generalize better across motions and environments. MARIO plugs into AirIO, TLIO, EqNIO and RoNIN-LSTM without changing their backbones, and the full AirIO variant runs at 315 FPS on a single GPU.

MethodA pose prior, a fusion layer, and the backbone you already have

PoseNet learns the body from one IMU

A CNN-GRU network maps head-IMU acceleration, angular velocity and orientation to SMPL joint positions and 6D joint rotations. Only the pelvis and legs are kept: they dominate global motion and are the joints a head sensor can actually constrain.

Auxiliary sensors become mid-level features

Barometric pressure is converted to altitude and vertical velocity, the magnetometer to a tilt-compensated heading, and the second IMU is kept as a raw 6-axis stream. Each is encoded by a small temporal CNN at the IMU rate.

Concatenate, fuse, hand off

The per-stream features are concatenated with the primary IMU features and passed through an MLP fusion layer. The result goes straight into AirIO, TLIO, EqNIO or RoNIN-LSTM, which predict velocity as usual.

Removing gravity from the raw accelerometer before encoding helps every variant (AirIO + All: 6.12 m → 4.64 m ATE). Mid-level feature fusion also outperforms early concatenation and cross-attention in our ablations. The paper reports the same pattern on Aria Everyday Activities and on the TLIO dataset.
